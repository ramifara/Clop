# Clop for Windows

A Windows port of Clop's automatic image, video, PDF, audio and clipboard workflow. This is an independent fork of [Clop by the Lowtech Guys](https://github.com/FuzzyIdeas/Clop), under the same GPLv3 license.

## The experience

Run Clop once. It stays in the system tray. There is no workbench, file browser, onboarding dashboard or sample-image screen; the first run shows one welcome card (see [First run](#first-run)).

Copy an image or screenshot in another app. Clop automatically optimises it, puts the result back on the clipboard and shows a small thumbnail card in the bottom-right corner. You can keep working in the original app. The card does not steal focus. Copied video, PDF and audio files are optimised the same way once their clipboard settings are on (see [Clipboard](#clipboard)).

The result uses the geometry and interaction model in the original `FloatingResult.swift`:

- A 196 × 148 thumbnail with the image filling its background.
- Size reduction and resolution over the bottom of the thumbnail.
- An 18-pixel format bar beneath it, with the current format preselected. Click PNG, JPG, WebP, AVIF or GIF to convert in place.
- Up to five small actions revealed on hover, chosen by `floatingResultActions`: by default downscale, restore or optimise, compression, aggressive optimisation and share. Copy, save and the rest are in the right-click menu.
- Downscaling opens a slider over the same thumbnail. Choose 100%, 75%, 50%, 25% or 10%, or use the slider. No separate editing window opens.
- Dimensions, `Crop and resize…`, `Compare (diff)` and Show in Explorer live in the right-click menu (also the `•••` button). Drag the thumbnail to another app.

While dragging, a transient 196 × 148 `Drop to optimise` target appears in the corner, matching `DropZone.swift`. Drop an image, video, PDF or audio file onto it to optimise. Releasing the drag elsewhere dismisses the target without changing the file. Dragged links from browsers can also be downloaded and optimised after an explicit drop.

The helper checks the object under the pointer at the original mouse press. In Explorer, the press must hit a selected image, video, PDF or audio file; it also recognises those files on the desktop. Other apps can expose image objects through Windows accessibility. A known unsupported image suffix is rejected. Text selection, text drags, title bars, resize handles and empty folder space stay quiet, even with an image selected or on the clipboard. Releasing the mouse or pressing Escape dismisses the target.

Apps that do not expose their image objects through Windows accessibility cannot reveal the target automatically. Copying their image still uses the automatic clipboard workflow. The tray's `Keep drop target visible` also provides a target for an explicit drop. The exact payload of an arbitrary app's drag is only available after a drop.

Images and videos saved to the Desktop are optimised where they are, with the original kept in the working directory (see [Watched folders](#watched-folders)).

Clipboard cards disappear after ten seconds and file cards after thirty, as in the original defaults. Hovering pauses dismissal. The tray's `Show latest results` brings recent cards back, and `Bring back last result` (`Ctrl+Shift+=`) shows the card that hid or was dismissed last. Up to three cards stack vertically. The tray also provides clipboard controls, optional pinning, settings and access to originals, `Pause automatic optimisations` (clipboard and watched folders), `Revert last optimisations` (the latest results get their originals back, over the watched files too) and `Stop all` (stops every running and queued optimisation; the files stay as they were); see [Tray menu](#tray-menu).

Settings stay closed unless requested from the tray (`Settings…`) or a `clop://settings` link; see [Settings](#settings). The current cursor's screen receives automatic popups.

## Install

Download the [Windows x64 installer](https://github.com/ramifara/Clop/releases/download/windows-v0.1.1/Clop-Windows-0.1.1-x64-Setup.exe) or [portable executable](https://github.com/ramifara/Clop/releases/download/windows-v0.1.1/Clop-Windows-0.1.1-x64-Portable.exe). Quit the previous version from its tray menu before upgrading. Windows 10 and Windows 11 are supported. The builds are unsigned, so Windows SmartScreen warns about a download from the releases page: choose `More info`, then `Run anyway`. A signing certificate is not decided yet. Releases are tagged `v<version>` from the next release on (the existing `windows-v0.1.1` tag is the last of the old format, which the updater cannot read); see [Updates](#updates). No Node.js, PowerShell module or development setup is required to use them.

## Image and clipboard details

Supported formats are PNG, JPEG, WebP, GIF, AVIF, HEIC, JPEG XL, BMP, SVG and single-page TIFF. TIFF and SVG become PNG (an SVG is rasterised at its document size); HEIC and BMP become JPEG, or PNG when they have transparent pixels, as macOS converts them by default. JPEG XL stays JPEG XL. HEIC and JPEG XL are read and written with libheif's `heif-dec`/`heif-enc` and libjxl's `djxl`/`cjxl`, BMP is read with ffmpeg. HDR photos (PQ or HLG, as iPhones, Android phones and HDR10 exports store them) are tone-mapped to SDR: light up to about three quarters of the HDR reference white (203 nits) keeps its brightness, and brighter light rolls off so the brightest pixel lands on white, which puts the reference white itself a little below white. Windows Clop writes no HDR and no gain maps, so in Lossless mode an HDR photo is kept as it is unless you scale, crop, watermark or convert it, which writes it as SDR. Animated GIF and WebP retain their frames and timing. Their format bar disables still-image targets. The tray app does not include an AI upscaler.

Every resize and format switch starts from the saved original. Restoring recovers its exact bytes. Dropped, opened and copied files are never overwritten; only files in watched folders are, with a backup. If a same-format, same-resolution optimisation would increase file size, Clop keeps the original bytes.

For an image result the Windows helper writes an encoded PNG, a Windows bitmap and a file-drop list together. File-aware apps receive optimised files; image-aware apps receive an image. Apps that only accept bitmap data may re-encode it themselves. Animation survives file paste and drag; bitmap paste uses the first frame. Copying something else during automatic processing prevents the old result from overwriting the new clipboard contents. Own writes do not trigger another optimisation.

Clop waits for the clipboard to settle before optimising, so an app that writes several times in quick succession costs one optimisation. Some editors, such as Snipping Tool with automatic copying, rewrite the clipboard after every brush stroke. When an app keeps writing images while it stays in front, Clop treats it as an editing session: it keeps only the latest version and optimises it once you switch to another app to paste. Snipping Tool starts in this mode. Copying text, switching apps or pausing for 30 seconds ends a session, so a normal copy, switch and paste is never delayed. `Ctrl+Shift+C` optimises the clipboard immediately at any time.

Images go through the same tools with the same arguments as on macOS: jpegoptim for JPEG, pngquant for PNG, gifsicle for GIF and ffmpeg for animated WebP. sharp only decodes, scales and converts, and encodes still WebP and AVIF at the macOS conversion quality; heif-enc writes HEIC at that quality and cjxl writes JPEG XL at the quality and effort macOS gives its JPEG XL encoder. The factor of the `imageCompression` setting sets each tool's arguments. Balanced uses the setting as it is (factor 30 by default). Smaller uses the macOS aggressive factor 64, or the setting itself when that is already 50 or more. From factor 80 GIFs lose every fourth frame, from 90 every third and from 98 every second; `gifFrameDropBehaviour` chooses whether they play faster or keep their duration. With the adaptive tier, Clop also tries PNG for a flat JPEG and JPEG for an opaque PNG, and keeps the other format when it is over 100 KB smaller. A downscaled PNG that would grow is requantized to fewer colours, or the original is kept. Balanced and Smaller compression may discard detail.

Lossless mode is Windows-only, because Clop on macOS has no lossless image tier. It changes no pixels: JPEG gets jpegoptim's lossless pass and GIF gifsicle's `-O3` without lossy compression; PNG, WebP, AVIF, HEIC and JPEG XL are re-encoded losslessly. Resizing a JPEG in Lossless mode produces PNG. A lossless HEIC keeps a 10- or 12-bit source's depth; a source deeper than 12 bits becomes PNG, since the HEVC encoder stops at 12. GIF holds only 256 colours, so in Lossless mode only an unedited GIF stays GIF; anything else that would become GIF is saved as PNG, or as WebP for an animation. JPEG conversion uses white behind transparent pixels.

`core/media/image-ops.ts` holds the image operations that pipelines and the CLI call, ported from macOS: crop to a crop size (a relative rectangle; an exact size filled around the centre, or with smart crop around sharp's attention region; a long edge; or an aspect ratio; GIFs and animations always keep the centre), watermark with another image in a corner or the centre at a scale and opacity (animations through ffmpeg, frame by frame), strip metadata, convert, and fit under a byte limit: the aggressive factor 64 first, then a binary search up to factor 100 for the gentlest factor that fits, then downscaling at factor 100.

With `stripMetadata` on (the default), results lose camera, location and author metadata but keep the original's resolution (EXIF and PNG `pHYs`, so the DPI survives conversion and scaling), orientation and, with `preserveColorMetadata`, their own colour profile. With it off, every tag of the original is copied, except the colour description, which always describes the result's pixels, and the orientation of re-encoded results, whose pixels are already upright.

Originals and results live in the working directory (see below) and are deleted after the `workdirCleanupInterval` setting, three days by default. Each result keeps its source's name, so a pasted or dragged file is named like the original. The app remembers up to 40 results during its session and automatically retires the oldest entries. Its local cache starts fresh on restart. Image limits are 128 MB, 60 million pixels across all frames and 250 animation frames. Downloads are limited to 128 MB, and a drop to 20 files.

## Videos, PDFs and audio in the app

Dropped, opened and copied videos, PDFs and audio files go through the engines below with the current settings: `videoCompression`, `videoEncoder`, the frame rate cap, `removeAudioFromVideos`, `convertAudioToAAC` and `stripMetadata` for video; `pdfDPI` for PDFs; `audioCompression`, `formatsToConvertToAAC`, `formatsToConvertToMP3` and `audioCoverArt` for audio. Their cards show a preview (a frame one second in, the first page, or the cover art; audio without art gets a drawn note), the size reduction and the duration or page count, with a percentage while ffmpeg or Ghostscript works. A card copies, saves, reveals, drags, restores and dismisses its result, and has the per-type actions from macOS (see [Result cards](#result-cards-right-click-menus-and-the-compact-list)). A copy of the original stays in the working directory, so a dropped or copied source file is never written to and restoring gives back its exact bytes. With `preserveDates` (on), optimised PDFs and audio keep the source's dates, as on macOS, where videos also get new ones. Each type has its own queue, and the clipboard keeps being read while a result is processed, so a long video never holds up a copied screenshot. Quitting Clop stops every running job.

## Result cards, right-click menus and the compact list

Every result, whatever its kind, has the same three ways to act on it, all built from one action table (`core/cards/actions.ts`): the corner buttons on a card, the buttons at the side of a compact row, and the right-click menu (a native Windows menu, built in the main process). The main process checks every action id and value the window sends against that table, per kind, before anything runs.

- **Corner buttons** come from `floatingResultActions` (at most five per card) and `compactResultActions` (at most nine), by kind: Downscale (a slider for images, a menu of factors for video, resolutions for PDFs, cover art sizes for audio), Compression, Crop and resize, Share, Restore or Optimise, Aggressive optimisation, Copy, Show in Explorer, Preview, Save as, Fit under size, and `pipeline:` buttons that run a saved pipeline. `addToShelf` and `sendSecurely` have no Windows counterpart and are not shown. `hideFloatingResultTooltips` hides the labels, `formatPickerStyle` chooses between the format bar and an extension chip that opens it, and videos, PDFs and audio show a progress bar while they work.
- **The right-click menu** (also the `•••` button) follows `RightClickMenu.swift`: Dismiss or Stop, Restore original, Preview, Compare; Save as, Copy, Show in Explorer; Open with the default app, `Edit with <app>` when `editorAppImage`, `editorAppVideo`, `editorAppPDF` or `editorAppAudio` is set, and `Open with…`; Crop and resize; Aggressive optimisation; Downscale, Compression and Convert submenus; Fit under size; Strip EXIF metadata; Pass file through pipeline (the saved pipelines for that kind). Per kind: video adds Remove audio, Convert HDR to SDR (only for an HDR clip), Change playback speed, Convert to GIF and Re-optimise with encoder; audio adds Change bitrate, Normalise loudness, Extract cover art (as a card of its own) and Downscale cover art; a PDF adds Reduce image resolution and Convert to image or Extract pages as images. Entries that cannot apply (no audio track to remove, a size the file is already under) are disabled. A video's Downscale and a video's or audio file's playback speed are totals, as on macOS: 50% then 80% leaves 80% of the original (a bigger size starts again from the original, as a video cannot get its pixels back), and 1x undoes a 2x.
- **Selecting several results** in the compact list (Ctrl-click, Shift-click) and right-clicking opens the batch menu: Save all to a folder, Copy, Restore all, Downscale, Change bitrate, Change playback speed, Aggressive optimisation, Convert HDR to SDR, Strip EXIF metadata and Dismiss. Each applies to the results it works on and skips the rest, including a video that is not HDR for Convert HDR to SDR or an audio file whose format lacks the chosen bitrate; only the results an action ran on go back on the clipboard. macOS's QuickLook, send and shelf entries for a selection are not offered. A selection also holds back the auto-clear.
- **Each action** runs through the engine that already does it: image downscale, compression and format changes re-optimise from the original with the card's options; the other actions run as pipeline steps (`stripExif`, `targetSize`, `removeAudio`, `changeSpeed`, `lowerBitrate`, `normalize`, `convert`, `optimise`) on the card's current file, or call the media function directly (HDR to SDR, video to GIF, cover art). A step that fails shows its message on the card, and Restore original brings the original back.
- **Preview** replaces QuickLook: a window with the current result at full size (videos and audio play, PDFs page by page). Space or Esc closes it.
- **Open with…** shows Windows' own chooser (`rundll32.exe shell32.dll,OpenAs_RunDLL`, started by its System32 path with the file as its only argument). **Edit with** fails closed: the editor setting must be the full path of an existing `.exe` (a bare name is never looked up in the Start menu or App Paths), not an interpreter or launcher such as `cmd`, `powershell` or `rundll32`, not inside a watched folder, not on a network share or a device path, and not marked by Windows as downloaded from the internet (its Zone.Identifier stream; one that cannot be read refuses too). Every check runs on the canonical path (links, junctions and short names resolved), and that path is the one started, with the file as its only argument and no shell. The menu hides the entry when the setting is not an existing `.exe` path, and the app refuses the action the same way if a window asks for it anyway.
- **Share** puts the file on the clipboard and selects it in Explorer, where Windows' Share command is. The system share sheet needs a packaged app identity, which Clop does not have, so this is the stand-in; Share never dismisses the card. `dismissFloatingResultOnUpload` and `dismissCompactResultOnUpload` belong to macOS's Dropshare upload and are not used here (the settings rows say so). `dismissFloatingResultOnDrop` and `dismissCompactResultOnDrop` dismiss a result dragged out of its card when the drag ends away from the results (Electron does not report whether the drop landed).
- **Compact list**: it replaces the cards with `alwaysShowCompactResults`, or when more results show than fit the screen (at most five cards), and stays until the shelf is empty. `showCompactImages` adds thumbnails. While the list shows, results do not hide one by one; `autoClearAllCompactResultsAfter` seconds after the last one finishes (0 never) they are all cleared, unless the pointer is on the list. `showCopyClearButtons` shows Copy all and Clear all.

| Key (on the card under the pointer) | Action |
| --- | --- |
| `Delete`, `Space`, `D` | Dismiss, Preview, Compare |
| `K`, `S`, `C` | Crop and resize, Save as, Copy |
| `R`, `A` | Restore original, Aggressive optimisation |

## Crop and compare windows

The right-click menu's `Crop and resize…` (images, videos and PDFs, not audio) and `Compare (diff)` (`Compare (A/B)` for audio, every type) each open a window of their own for that result, ported from `CropWindow.swift` and `CompareView.swift`. Each result has at most one of each, and opening it again brings it forward. Both close with their result, and `Escape` closes them.

`Crop and resize…` shows the original (a video frame you choose with the slider, or a PDF page) with a selection you can draw, move and resize. Pick an aspect ratio, a saved size or a typed size, flip the orientation, and click `Crop to W×H` or press `Ctrl+Enter`. Images and videos are always cropped from the original, so cropping again, downscaling or converting keeps the crop and `Restore original` drops it; the card's downscale then applies to the area the crop kept. A PDF is cropped on its current result instead: the window offers paper sizes, device sizes and aspect ratios, which either crop each page by its own orientation or extend it with empty paper, and shows each page whole with its current crop box as the starting selection. If a crop fails the window stays open and shows the error.

Saved crop sizes are the `savedCropSizes` setting in `settings.json`, the macOS defaults at first. The window can save the current size under a name (up to 200 are kept), delete a saved size or bring the defaults back. Sizes are whole pixels up to 16383, and a side of 0 means automatic.

`Compare (diff)` (`Compare (A/B)` for audio) shows the item's retained original beside its current result, never the live file. `Split` drags a divider across both, `Side by side` shows them next to each other; holding `Ctrl` zooms both alike (`Alt` as well zooms further) and the cursor pans. Videos play together, muted and looping, with a shared pause (`Space`). A PDF compares one whole page at a time (the arrow keys or the slider change page). Audio files get a player each, with only one playing at a time and a shared start position that scrubbing sets. Images the window cannot show as they are (HEIC, JPEG XL, BMP, TIFF) are shown as PNG. An open window follows its result: a downscale, restore or new crop replaces what it shows.

The windows are sandboxed and see only a narrow preload API. They never name a file: the main process hands out `clop-media://file/<token>` URLs, one opaque token per file, only for files of the session (the originals, results and previews Clop keeps in its working directory). The protocol is session-only, answers just `GET` and `HEAD` with byte ranges so videos can seek, does not follow a link out of the session, and forgets a result's files when it is dismissed.

Known gaps against macOS: the compare window has no file-actions menu or path field, and the crop window neither remembers its size and position nor closes itself when its result starts processing again. The native window opening, the crop selection's pointer handling and DPI behaviour have been exercised only in a browser preview and unit tests, not yet on Windows hardware.

## Clipboard

Automatic clipboard optimisation follows `handleClipboardChange` in `ClopApp.swift`. Copied files of each type are optimised only when that type's setting is on: `optimiseVideoClipboard`, `optimisePDFClipboard` and `optimiseAudioClipboard` (all off by default, as on macOS, and in Settings). Image files copied in Explorer are optimised with `optimiseImagePathClipboard`, which is on by default on Windows, as the Windows app always did; macOS defaults it to off because a copied file is only a reference. Image data from an app is always optimised, including the file some apps put beside it. Files in `imageFormatsToSkip` (TIFF by default), `videoFormatsToSkip` (MKV and M4V) and `audioFormatsToSkip` are left alone. A copied absolute path on a local drive (`C:\…` or a `file:` URL, quoted or not) counts as a copy of that file. Network and device paths (`\\server\share\…`, `\\?\…`, `file://server/…`), copied as text or as files, are only followed by `Ctrl+Shift+C`, because any app can put one on the clipboard and looking one up makes Windows contact that server with your credentials. Files copied from a mapped network drive (`Z:\…`) are optimised automatically like local ones; for a share opened by its `\\server` path a notice says to press `Ctrl+Shift+C`. Automatic optimisation checks a path's type and settings before it touches the file system at all. Content marked `ExcludeClipboardContentFromMonitorProcessing`, as password managers mark it, is never read, not even by `Ctrl+Shift+C`, and while the Windows helper is not running nothing is read, since the mark cannot be checked.

The result goes back on the clipboard as a file list holding the optimised file under its source's name (a copied list's other entries, and files past the 20 it optimises at once, stay in it unchanged), with image data for images and the path as text for a copied path. `Ctrl+Shift+C` optimises whatever is on the clipboard whatever these settings say: any copied media file or path, image data, a data-URL image (`data:image/png;base64,…`) or bare base64 image data and an http(s) link, which is downloaded. Links are only downloaded then or when dropped; Clop never fetches a copied link by itself. OneDrive files-on-demand are downloaded by reading them; a file that cannot be downloaded is skipped with a notice.

A new clipboard image replaces the previous clipboard image's card, so `Show latest results` brings back only the latest clipboard image unless `appendClipboardResults` is on. With `appendClipboardResults` the cards stay, until `clipboardAccumulationTimeout` seconds (30) pass without a new one, and with `copyConsecutiveClipboardImages` all of them go on the clipboard together as files. `copyImageFilePath` (on) puts an image result's file beside its image data; off, only the image data goes on the clipboard. With `copyImageFilePath` and `useCustomNameTemplateForClipboardImages` on, clipboard images are named by `customNameTemplateForClipboardImages` (`clop_%y-%m-%d_%i` when empty). Clipboard results always go back on the clipboard, as on macOS; `autoCopyToClipboard` decides whether dropped and opened files' results do too. All of these options are in the Clipboard and Images tabs of the settings window.

Copies made by an app in `clipboardIgnoredAppBundleIds` are not optimised automatically; `Ctrl+Shift+C` still optimises them. The app is the clipboard's owner (the app in front when the clipboard has no owner), and an entry is its exe path, its AUMID for a packaged app (such as `Microsoft.ScreenSketch_8wekyb3d8bbwe!App`), or just an exe name such as `KeePassXC.exe`, compared without regard to case. The settings window picks apps from those running with a window and the Start Menu's programs and apps.

## Watched folders

Clop watches `imageDirs` and `videoDirs` (the Desktop by default, as on macOS), `pdfDirs` and `audioDirs` (none by default), including their subfolders, and optimises each file of that type that appears, is renamed or copied in, or gets new content, once its size and modification time have held for a second and no other app holds it open for writing, as `FileOptimisationWatcher.swift` does. A change to a file's attributes alone (read-only, archive, last access, OneDrive pinning) is not new content; a copy that replaces a file is, even when it keeps the copied file's older modification time, once Clop has seen the file before. Each watched folder has one watch for its whole tree (one ReadDirectoryChangesW handle), shared by every type watching it; links and junctions inside it are not followed. When Windows reports that changes were lost because too many came at once, Clop looks again at files changed in the last minute, once per folder for every type. Each watched folder is watched on its own, so one that cannot be watched does not stop the others. A watched folder that is renamed, moved to the Recycle Bin or replaced by another of the same name is watched again where its path leads, and one that cannot be watched is retried less and less often, up to every five minutes, with one notice. `enableAutomaticImageOptimisations`, `…Video…`, `…PDF…` (on) and `…Audio…` (off) switch each type, and nothing is watched while `pauseAutomaticOptimisations` is on. Files that change in the first seconds, before the Windows helper has started, wait for it and are then looked at; the helper is what tells a cloud-only OneDrive placeholder (which reading would download) from a local file. If the helper has stopped or cannot start, new files in watched folders are left alone and a notice says so. A watched folder that does not exist yet, such as one on a drive that is not connected, is checked for every few seconds and watched once it appears.

Files already in a folder when watching starts are left alone, and so are hidden files and folders, Clop's own temporary copies and working directory, OneDrive files-on-demand whose content is not downloaded (reading them would download them), files Clop already optimised (the `optimised.json` marker; a file renamed or moved after optimising, by its stream hint while its size is unchanged; and any file Clop wrote in the last `optimisedFileProtectionMs`), formats in `…FormatsToSkip`, files outside `min…SizeKB` and `max…SizeMB`, images and videos outside `min…Resolution` and `max…Resolution`, and files a `.clopignore-image` (`-video`, `-pdf`, `-audio`) file in the watched folder names with gitignore-style patterns. When more than `max…FileCount` files of a type appear at once (4 images, 1 video, 2 PDFs or 2 audio files by default), none of them is optimised and a notice says so. During the first 30 seconds of the very first launch, files that change are held for three seconds; more than five means another app keeps rewriting them, and Clop turns that type off.

A watched file's result goes where `optimised…Behaviour` says (see [Working directory, placement and names](#working-directory-placement-and-names)); by default it replaces the file, whose original moves into `backups`. The result shows as a card unless its folder is in `dirsHideFloatingResult`. Restoring the card, or `Revert last optimisations`, copies the original back and marks it, so it is not optimised again, and gives back any file of yours that a result took the place of. A result you edited, moved or deleted since is left as it is, with a notice; its original stays in `backups`, and the rest of the batch is still reverted. `defaultImageFormat` does not apply to watched files. The batch optimiser (`useBatchModeForFolders`) does not run on watched folders yet; their pipelines do (see [Pipelines](#pipelines)). A drop with the aggressive modifier skips the drop zone's pipelines, as on macOS.

## Video engine

`core/media/video.ts` processes video with ffmpeg and ffprobe using the macOS arguments. The app optimises videos with it; pipelines and the CLI will use its other operations. It optimises, scales, crops, changes speed, caps the frame rate, removes audio and converts to HEVC, AV1 or VP9 WebM, all in one ffmpeg pass. It also makes GIFs through gifski, at 960 pixels wide and 15 fps by default. Progress comes from ffmpeg's `-progress` output.

The `videoCompression` tier picks the encoder. `fast` uses a hardware encoder when one works and libx264 at the `veryfast` preset otherwise. `smaller` and custom factors use libx264 at the CRF and preset the factor maps to, and `lossless` uses CRF 17. `adaptive` chooses between them per file, as Apple Silicon Macs do, and aggressive mode uses `veryslow` at CRF 28. With `videoEncoder` set to `auto`, Clop tries NVIDIA NVENC, Intel Quick Sync, AMD AMF and Media Foundation in that order. Each candidate must encode a test frame, and the first that works is used for the rest of the session. Naming an encoder forces it, falling back to libx264 or libx265 when it does not work. Naming an HEVC encoder makes every tier encode HEVC. Hardware encoders take the software CRF as their constant-quality level.

Plain optimisations that come out larger keep the original; removing audio or asking for SDR always produces a new file. Failed ffmpeg and gifski runs are retried up to three times, as on macOS. Speed changes re-time the audio with `atempo`, which macOS leaves at its original length. HDR video (PQ or HLG) is tone-mapped to SDR when the result is H.264. HEVC, AV1 and VP9 conversions keep HDR unless SDR is requested. With `stripMetadata` on, results lose their title, location and other container metadata.

## PDF engine

`core/media/pdf.ts` holds the PDF engine, which the app uses to optimise PDFs. Ghostscript compresses with the argument sets from `PDF.swift`. Below 300 DPI it downsamples and re-encodes images as JPEG, with lower JPEG quality at 100, 72 and 48 DPI. At 300 DPI it passes JPEGs through untouched. The `pdfDPI` setting is 0 (adaptive) or a fixed DPI from 48 to 300. Adaptive mode estimates each image's DPI from its pixel size over its page's size, as macOS does. That estimate assumes each image fills its page. Images that read abnormally low are dropped as outliers. Clop then picks the highest stop (300, 250, 200, 150, 100, 72, 48) with more than three images at or above it, and 300 when there are no images. Aggressive mode goes one stop lower. An explicit DPI from a pipeline or the CLI is used as given. PDFs over 150 pages are split into 100-page chunks, and the chunks are merged afterwards. No more than four Ghostscript processes run at once across all PDF jobs. A failed Ghostscript run is retried, up to three tries for a whole PDF and two per chunk. As on macOS, the merge keeps pages but drops document outlines and links that point into another chunk. A result that is not smaller keeps the original. Encrypted PDFs are refused.

Cropping to an aspect ratio or a paper size (`core/data/paperSizes.ts`, the macOS table) changes only each page's CropBox. The MediaBox keeps the full page, so uncropping restores it. Extending grows the MediaBox instead and keeps all content. Page boxes are edited with [`@cantoo/pdf-lib`](https://github.com/cantoo-scribe/pdf-lib), a maintained fork of pdf-lib. Pages render through Ghostscript (`png16m`, or JPEG at quality 90) at 144 DPI within the CropBox. PNG pages come out on an opaque white background. macOS leaves the background of PNG pages transparent. They can then go through the image optimiser, and are named `<name>-page<n>.png` or `.jpg`.

## Audio engine

`core/media/audio.ts` processes audio with ffmpeg using the macOS arguments. The app optimises audio files with it. It optimises, lowers the bitrate, changes speed, converts between AAC (M4A), MP3, Opus (OGG), FLAC, WAV and AIFF, normalises loudness and handles cover art. Progress comes from ffmpeg's `-progress` output. The input's bitrate is the audio stream's own; for FLAC and Ogg files with cover art, whose streams do not state one, Clop measures the audio packets so the art does not count.

The factor of the `audioCompression` setting picks the bitrate: 256 down to 48 kbps for AAC, 320 to 64 for MP3 and 160 to 32 for Opus, in steps of 16. The default factor 35 gives 192 kbps. The bitrate is capped at the input's and snapped down to a standard bitrate, so a file is never re-encoded at a higher one, and aggressive mode goes one step below the input. MP3 uses LAME's VBR quality levels. AAC uses ffmpeg's own encoder instead of Apple's, so sizes differ slightly from macOS. WAV and AIFF become 16-bit at no more than 48 kHz, and aggressive WAV becomes IMA ADPCM. Formats listed in `formatsToConvertToAAC` (AIFF and FLAC by default) and `formatsToConvertToMP3` (WAV) are converted; other files keep their format. A result in the same format that is not smaller keeps the original, and so does lowering the bitrate to a target that is not below the input's.

Speed changes chain `atempo` filters, so any factor works. As on macOS they drop the cover art. They keep the input's codec (Ogg Opus stays Opus) at the encoder's default bitrate. Loudness normalisation uses single-pass `loudnorm` with a true peak of -1.5 dB and resamples back to the input's rate.

The `audioCoverArt` setting chooses what happens to artwork in M4A, MP3 and FLAC files. `optimise` (the default) recompresses JPEG art with jpegoptim at quality 68 and PNG art with pngquant, and keeps photographic PNG art as JPEG when that is smaller. `keep` copies the art untouched and `remove` drops it. Opus, WAV and AIFF results never carry art. Optimised art can also be cropped to a square (landscape art only, or always) and capped at a long edge. Art can be extracted as it is, or downscaled and re-embedded without re-encoding the audio.

## Working directory, placement and names

The working directory is `%APPDATA%\Clop for Windows\work` unless the `workdir` setting says otherwise (an absolute path; `~` and `$HOME` mean your profile folder; an empty or relative value is refused and Clop uses the default folder). A new `workdir` applies the next time Clop starts. It holds these folders:

- `backups`: the original of every file Clop replaces in place, named `<name>-<hash>.<ext>`. Restoring copies it back byte for byte and keeps the original modification time.
- `batch-backups`: one `batch-<id>` folder per batch (see [Batch optimiser](#batch-optimiser)). Nothing here is ever deleted automatically, because it can be the only copy of a file.
- `temp`: the running session's originals and results (`session-*`) and other scratch files.
- `placing`: a note for each original that is away from its path while a result takes its place (a conversion over the file, or a file Windows will not let Clop rename over). Quitting, installing an update and signing out or shutting down wait for placements under way (quitting for up to two minutes); if Clop is ended anyway, the next start copies each such original back from `backups` and says so.

Clop only uses a folder that is empty, that it made before (it leaves a `.clop-workdir` file there) or that holds nothing but its own folders, and it refuses a path with a link (symlink or junction) in it. Otherwise it reports the problem and uses the default folder. Every 10 minutes Clop deletes files in `backups` and `temp` that have not changed for longer than `workdirCleanupInterval` (10 minutes to 1 month, or never) and removes the folders that empty out. A file's age counts from when it arrived (its change or creation time), not from its own modification time, which a backup keeps; on FAT and exFAT drives, where a move updates neither, a backup moved in is copied afresh so it does not look old. The running session's folder is kept until the next start. The older `%APPDATA%\Clop for Windows\images` folder is not migrated. It ages out with the same interval and disappears once empty. `core/workdir.ts` also provides `forceClean()`, which empties `backups` and `temp` but not `batch-backups` or the running session.

`core/placement.ts` ports `FilePlacement.swift`. For each file type and kind (optimised, automatic conversion, manual conversion) the `optimised*Behaviour`, `converted*Behaviour` and `manualConverted*Behaviour` settings choose one of four modes:

- `temporary`: the result stays in the working directory and the original is untouched.
- `inPlace`: the original moves into `backups` and the result takes its place, with the result's extension. If the backup cannot be made, nothing is replaced.
- `sameFolder`: the result is written next to the original under `sameFolderNameTemplate*` (default `%f-optimised`).
- `specificFolder`: the result goes to the path made by `specificFolderNameTemplate*` (default `%P/optimised/%f`), creating folders as needed.

If another file already sits at the destination, it is copied into `backups` first and the result reports it as `replaced`. Folders that come from `%P` or `%F` keep their names exactly; only the literal parts of a template and the file name are made safe.

A file that already sits at its templated location is left there, so templates never stack (`a-optimised-optimised.png`). Copies go through a temporary file in the destination folder and replace the target in one rename, retrying while Defender or a preview handler holds the file. There are no copy-on-write clones on Windows; every backup is a full copy.

Name templates (`core/template.ts`) use `%y` year, `%m` month, `%n` month name, `%d` day, `%w` weekday (1 is Sunday), `%H` hour, `%M` minutes, `%S` seconds, `%p` AM or PM, `%r` five random letters, `%i` auto-incrementing number (`lastAutoIncrementingNumber`), `%f` file name, `%e` extension, `%P` folder and `%F` full path. `~`, `$HOME` and a leading `%USERPROFILE%` mean your profile folder. As on macOS, file names replace the characters `/ : { } < > * | $ # & ^ ; ' "`, the backtick and control characters with `_`. Windows adds `\` and `?`, a trailing dot or space, and reserved device names such as `CON` and `NUL`, which get a `_`. As on macOS, `%p` reads AM from 12:00 to 12:59.

Optimised files are remembered in `%APPDATA%\Clop for Windows\optimised.json`, keyed by path with the file's size and modification time. A file counts as optimised only while both still match, so editing it or saving it through a temporary file clears the mark. Entries older than 30 days or whose file no longer exists are dropped when the app starts, and the list holds at most 50,000 files. On NTFS Clop also writes a `clop.optimisation.status` alternate data stream, with the file's size and modification time, as a hint that follows a renamed or moved file. It counts only while both still match, so any edit clears it. Zip, FAT, OneDrive sync and many editors lose it, so nothing relies on it.

## Pipelines

A pipeline is a list of steps written as `step(param: value, …) -> step -> …`, the same language as Clop for macOS, for example `if(regex: "^screenshot") -> crop(longEdge: 1600) -> convert(to: webp)`. Steps are separated by `->` or a line break, even inside quotes, so a quoted value cannot contain `->`. Quoted values have no escapes. `core/pipeline/` holds the model, the parser, the built-in library and the executor. The app runs them as Clop for macOS does: pipelines attached to the clipboard, the drop zone or a watched folder run on what arrives there, and a card's ••• menu has "Run pipeline…" with the saved pipelines for its type. `clop pipeline run` runs them from a terminal.

- `model.ts` mirrors `Pipeline` and `PipelineStep` exactly as Swift's Codable stores them, so the `savedPipelines`, `pipelinesToRunOn*` and `presetZones` settings move between macOS and Windows unchanged. `codec.ts` reads them as objects or as the JSON strings macOS keeps in its defaults, and writes them back byte for byte as macOS writes them (`fixtures/pipelines/macos-store.json` is a hand-written store covering every step). A pipeline's `steps` are what runs; `rawText` is only parsed when `steps` is missing or empty. Reading checks only what the macOS decoder checks, so a pipeline macOS stores is never lost or rewritten: values Windows cannot run (a range macOS allows, an ICU regex) are kept and reported as warnings, values a newer macOS added are kept, and an entry this version cannot read at all (a step from a newer macOS) stays in the settings file exactly as it came and syncs back unchanged. It just does not run here.
- `parser.ts` turns text into steps and steps back into text. As on macOS, a quote starts a quoted value only as the value's first character (`rename(to: Rami's copy)` needs no quotes), and `removeAudio` is written without parentheses. Where macOS silently drops a step it cannot read, text typed on Windows gets each problem with its line and column: an unknown step or parameter (with a suggestion), a missing or repeated parameter, a value of the wrong kind or out of range, and, for a pipeline with a file type, a step, parameter or value that does not work on that type. Paths inside the profile folder are stored as `~\…` or `~/…`. Some stored steps have no exact text form (a value with `->` or a line break, a Shortcut identifier, a value only macOS accepts); `canFormat` says so, so an editor can show them as JSON.
- `builtins.ts` holds the eleven built-ins from macOS (`to WebP`, `Sort screenshots`, `0.5×`, `Watermark`, `1080p`, `to GIF`, `2× silent`, `as images`, `to MP3` and the per-type duplicates) with the same ids, texts and versions. They are added to `savedPipelines` once per version (`builtinPipelinesSeededVersion`), so a deleted built-in stays deleted. `~/Pictures/Screenshots` is `%USERPROFILE%\Pictures\Screenshots`, where Windows saves screenshots too.
- `executor.ts` runs a pipeline on one file, headless. Each step works on the file the step before it left. As on macOS, consecutive processing and media steps run as one pass and only the pass's result is placed, so `crop -> convert(to: webp)` leaves the original alone; a step whose location is not `inPlace` ends its pass. A step's `location` places its result: `inPlace` replaces the original, which is kept in the work directory's backups first; `sameFolder` writes next to it; `temporaryFolder` leaves it in the work directory; anything else is a path template. A `convert` without a location follows the manual conversion setting. Placed results are marked optimised. A processing step that does not apply to the file type is skipped. A filter that does not match ends the pipeline quietly. Unlike macOS, which logs most failed steps and carries on with the file it had, a step that fails ends the pipeline with an error that names the step, or the range of steps that ran as one pass. A stored step with a value out of range or empty (`downscale(factor: 0)`, `rename(to: "")`) is refused before anything runs.
- What runs differently on Windows: `runScript` code runs in Windows PowerShell, from an empty temporary folder. A script file gets the file as its first argument: a `.ps1` through PowerShell, an `.exe` or `.com` directly, a `.bat` or `.cmd` through `cmd`. Other files (`.js`, `.vbs`, shortcuts, files without an extension) are refused, since Windows would run them through whatever program their type is associated with. `cmd` runs with code page 65001 (UTF-8), so a batch file saved in an ANSI or OEM code page reads its non-ASCII text wrongly; save batch files as UTF-8. Both kinds see `CLOP_INPUT_FILE` and `CLOP_BIN`, and a printed absolute path of the same kind of file replaces the file; one printed line that names no such file is reported as a warning. Output is read as UTF-8 (Windows-1252 when it is not valid UTF-8); a `.ps1` that prints paths with non-ASCII characters should first run `[Console]::OutputEncoding = [Text.UTF8Encoding]::new($false)`. Whoever starts the run decides whether scripts may run. Since a pipeline can run on whatever lands in a watched folder, nothing in that folder decides what runs: a script path must be absolute once its tokens are expanded (`%P/fix.ps1` and `~/scripts/fix.ps1` are), a script runs from its own folder, and a script Windows marks as downloaded from the internet is refused until it is unblocked. Clop itself starts only programs from System32 by their full path, and the script the pipeline names; commands a `.bat` or `.cmd` runs by name are looked up on `PATH` without the current folder, but what a script does beyond that is up to it. Nothing inside a watched folder runs: an `.exe` or `.com` there is refused because a program loads DLLs from its own folder and a file arriving there could run inside it, and a script (`.ps1`, `.bat` or `.cmd`) is refused when it sits in a watched folder that is being watched right now (its type is switched on and automatic optimisations are not paused) and the file it runs on is in that same folder, because files arrive in a watched folder unmarked (synced, unzipped, copied from a share). So `runScript(path: "%P/fix.ps1")` on a watched folder's pipeline does not run; keep scripts in a folder Clop does not watch. A script in the Desktop folder still runs for a clipboard or drop-zone pipeline, or a file that is somewhere else. Tokens such as `%f` or `$1` in a script path let the arriving file's name choose which script in that folder runs, so keep only scripts you mean to run there. `runShortcut(name:)` runs the script of that name (`.ps1`, `.bat`, `.cmd`, `.exe` or `.com`) from `%APPDATA%\Clop for Windows\scripts`, and nowhere else. `shelveWith` and `uploadWith` open the file with the named app, like `openWith`. `copyLinkForSending` fails with "Share links need an upload target in Settings". `copy(to:)` and `move(to:)` mark the file they make as optimised, so a watcher over the folder it lands in does not run its pipelines on it again; that also means the file is not optimised by that folder's watcher. Every result in the shelf is marked the same way (on Windows a stream on the file that Explorer copies and drags carry), so pasting or dragging one into a watched folder does not optimise it a second time. `delete` moves to the Recycle Bin. `delete(path: "sourceFile")`, the default, deletes the file the pipeline started with, as the macOS pipeline reference describes; the macOS code itself never resolves that word to a file, so there it deletes nothing. `if(regex:)` reads the ICU patterns macOS writes: leading flags such as `(?i)`, `\Q…\E`, `\A` and `\z` are translated, and other ICU-only syntax (possessive quantifiers such as `a++`, atomic groups, nested or POSIX classes, `&&`, `\x{…}`, `\h`) stops the step with an error instead of matching something else. `if(types:)` also takes whole kinds (`image`, `video`, `audio`, `pdf`). `optimise(encoder: lossless)` on an image uses the lossless tier.
- In the app (`electron/pipelines.ts`): pipelines attached to `clipboard`, `dropZone` or a watched folder run after Clop's usual optimisation, on the optimised file. When every attached pipeline skips optimisation ("skip optimisation" set, or a first step that re-encodes: convert, crop, downscale), they run instead of it, on the original: a watched file where it is, a copied or dropped file as a copy in the shelf. If none of them then does anything (a filter stopped it), a copied or dropped file is still optimised, while a watched file is left alone and its card stays out of sight. As on macOS, only a copied image goes straight to such pipelines: a copied video, PDF or audio file is optimised first and its pipelines run on the result. The card follows the pipelines' final file, lists their warnings and failures, and a pipeline that failed does not stop the next one. `hideResult` keeps the card out of the floating results, and a forked file gets a card of its own. The built-in pipelines are added to the library at startup, once per version. `openWith` (and `shelveWith`, `uploadWith`) opens only an app Windows knows: one running, one in the Start menu (packaged apps included), or an App Paths entry; a program by its full path only from the user's own pipelines, and never from a watched folder. Without script permission (an agent's pipeline, or `clop` run by an agent) it also refuses interpreters and launchers (`cmd`, `powershell`, `pwsh`, `wscript`, `cscript`, `mshta`, `rundll32`, `regsvr32`, `msiexec`, `python`, `node`, `bash`, `wsl`, `explorer`, `schtasks`, `certutil`, `msbuild`, `wmic` and similar), whether they are named or an App Paths or Start menu entry leads to them. `delete` asks before it moves a folder to the Recycle Bin. Without the app (a headless `clop pipeline run`) nobody can be asked, so a folder is refused unless a person passes `--delete-folders`; an agent's pipeline never deletes a folder headless. A `copyToClipboard` step does not overwrite something the user copied while the pipeline ran. Temporary results a card shows are kept from the work directory cleaner while Clop runs.
- `triggers.ts` has `pipelinesFor(type, source, settings)`, which returns the pipelines attached to a watched folder or to `clipboard` in `pipelinesToRunOn*`, with references to saved pipelines resolved. A folder attached as `~/Downloads` matches its full path, and on Windows any case and slash direction match.
- `templates.ts` is the step and parameter catalogue for autocomplete, with the template variables, and `reference.ts` is the DSL reference that `clop pipeline prompt` prints. On Windows, `runScript` code is PowerShell, and `runShortcut`, `shelveWith` and `uploadWith` name macOS apps.
- `complete.ts`, `highlight.ts`, `jsonc.ts` and `editor-state.ts` are the editor's logic, free of React: autocomplete from `templates.ts` (step names for the pipeline's file type, then parameters not yet given, then values, then template variables inside quotes or after `%`; typing a letter after a closed step inserts the `->`), syntax tokens and error underlines, the JSONC view (the steps one per line, a `//` comment with each step's text form, comments allowed when read back) and the state the window shows. `editor-commands.ts` holds the commands the editor window may send.
- The pipeline editor (`src/Editor.tsx`) opens from the tray's `Pipelines…` in its own sandboxed window. **Pipelines** lists the library, with the built-ins badged and editable, a Text view that highlights, completes and underlines each parse problem at its line and column as you type (Tab or Enter on a chosen suggestion completes, Ctrl+Enter saves) and a JSON view, which is also the only way to edit a step `canFormat` rejects. An entry this version cannot read is shown with its reason; fixing its JSON replaces it, and nothing else touches it. **Attached** lists, per file type, the clipboard, the drop zone and each watched folder of that type, with the pipelines attached and what can still be attached (a pipeline limited to one file type attaches only there). **Preset zones** adds, renames, reorders and removes the zones. Deleting a pipeline detaches it everywhere and removes the preset zones that ran it.
- The window never writes settings. It sends one of a few commands (`pipeline-save`, `pipeline-fix`, `pipeline-delete`, `attach`, `detach`, `zone-save`, `zone-delete`, `zone-move`) over `clop:editor`, which the main process accepts only from that window. `EditorService` checks each command's shape, parses the text or JSON again itself (the window's parse is only for showing errors) and applies commands one at a time, so none overwrite another. A malformed command is refused whole. Entries that Windows cannot read are written back exactly as they came. The settings the window changes are the ones the settings file already holds, so macOS reads them unchanged.
- Preset zones (`zones.ts`, `PresetZones.swift`): while a drag is on, the Windows helper reports whether Ctrl or Alt is held (polled every 40 ms on its own timer, never from inside the mouse hook), and either one reveals up to four tiles beside the drop target: the zones for the dragged file type first, then those for any type, then a `+` tile that opens the editor on **Preset zones** while there is room. Tiles are in two columns; `zones.ts` finds the tile under the pointer, gaps included. Dropping on a tile optimises the file, then runs that zone's pipeline, which is looked up again in the library in the main process (a deleted, unreadable, empty or wrong-type pipeline is refused). Dropping elsewhere on the target runs the pipelines attached to the drop zone. The tiles stay for the rest of the drag once shown, so letting go of Ctrl does not lose a tile under the pointer.
- To see the editor without Electron, run `npm run dev` and open `http://127.0.0.1:5173/?editor`; `CLOP_PREVIEW_DEMO=1 npm run dev` fills it with example pipelines, attachments and zones (`scripts/preview-demo.ts`). The preview keeps the settings in memory only.

## Settings

The settings window has the sixteen tabs of the macOS app: General, Clipboard, File handling, Video, Audio, Images, PDF, Drop Zone, Preset Zones, Floating Results, Keyboard Shortcuts, Pipelines, Automation, MCP, Updates (the macOS License & updates tab, renamed: there is no licence to enter) and About. The search field in the sidebar (`Ctrl+F`) finds rows by their title, subtitle and keywords, tolerating inflections and typos; it is a port of `SettingsSearchIndex.swift`. `Ctrl+1` to `Ctrl+9` and `Ctrl+Tab` switch tabs. `clop://settings/<tab>[/<row>]`, `clop://settings/<row>` and `?highlight=<row>` open the window on a tab and flash a row. A row is named by its id (`general.optimisation.stripMetadata`, as on macOS) or by the setting it writes (`stripMetadata`), and it opens on the tab it lives on whatever tab the link names.

`core/settings/entries.ts` describes every row once (title, subtitle, search keywords, tab, section, and the control to draw if the setting's type does not say), and the window lays each tab out from it, drawing each control from the schema's type (switch, choice, number, text, format chips) or from the row's hint (compression, size and resolution ranges, name templates, watched folders, shortcut recorder). A test checks that every row names settings that exist, that every supported setting has a row, and that searching a row's title finds it. Settings the window never shows are listed in `HIDDEN_KEYS` with the reason (state kept for the app, legacy flags); settings macOS marks `unsupportedOnWindows` (iCloud sync, Photos, the menubar icon styles, licence prompts) are hidden. Windows-only settings sit with the feature they belong to: starting with Windows and the debug log in General, the video encoder in Video, the default image format in Images, the pinned drop zone in Drop Zone, and keeping results on top in Floating Results.

The window can write only the settings it has rows for (`RENDERER_SETTINGS` in `electron/settings.ts`, checked value by value). These change only through checked actions of the main process, so a compromised page could not point Clop at a program, folder or script: the editor programs (a program picker; on Windows only an `.exe`), the working directory and watched folders (a folder picker; the working directory applies after a restart), the ignored-apps list (the Windows helper's app list or a program picker), the MCP switches (the MCP actions, which ask before scripts are allowed), and the saved pipelines, preset zones and what runs on each source (the pipeline editor; the Pipelines, Automation and Preset Zones tabs show them, and their button opens the editor window). Name and path templates have a live preview that the main process makes with `core/template.ts`, so it is the name Clop will really give a file.

The keyboard shortcuts tab stores trigger keys, action keys and resize keys as Electron accelerators, which Clop registers as global shortcuts (see [Shortcuts](#shortcuts)). A change registers and releases only the shortcuts that differ, and the window refuses trigger keys that would take over typing (none, Shift alone, Ctrl alone, Alt alone, the Windows key alone or Ctrl+Alt), combinations Windows keeps for itself, and resize keys that are not the number keys 1 to 9.

Some settings have rows but nothing in Clop for Windows reads them yet. They stay editable, so a value you set is kept (and imported from macOS settings), search finds them, and the row says "Not used by Clop for Windows yet" (a section where nothing works yet shows that once, as a banner). A test pins the list, so it shrinks on purpose as features arrive:

- Floating results: `enableFloatingResults`, `followCursorScreen`, `dismissFloatingResultOnUpload`, `dismissCompactResultOnUpload` (no upload on Windows), `autoHideFloatingResults`, `autoHideFloatingResultsAfter`, `autoHideClipboardResultAfter` (the other floating-result settings are read by the cards and the compact list, see [Result cards](#result-cards-right-click-menus-and-the-compact-list)).
- Clipboard and conversion: `optimiseTIFF`, `optimiseHEICAVIFClipboard`, `convertHDRToSDR`, `formatsToConvertToJPEG`, `formatsToConvertToPNG`, `formatsToConvertToMP4`, `formatsToConvertToOutputAudio`.
- Elsewhere: `defaultLinkExpiration`, `onlyShowDropZoneOnOption`, `onlyShowPresetZonesOnControlTapped`.

## Command line

The installer puts `clop` on your PATH (the `cli` folder in the install folder) and takes it off again when you uninstall. Uninstalling also removes the `clop://` link handler and the `launchAtLogin` entry the app adds itself, when they start the uninstalled app. Open a new terminal after installing. The portable app has `Install command-line tool` in its tray menu instead; it copies `clop.exe` to `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Clop for Windows\cli` and adds that folder. The portable app unpacks itself to a new temporary folder each time it starts, so with the portable app `clop` works only while the app is running; otherwise it says to start it.

The commands, subcommands and flags are the same as on macOS: `optimise` (with `image`, `video`, `pdf`, `audio` or mixed files), `crop`, `downscale`, `convert image|video|audio` and the older `convert -f avif|heic|webp`, `crop-pdf`, `uncrop-pdf`, `strip-exif`, `pipeline list|show|run|add|delete|prompt|attach|detach|preset add|remove`, `settings schema|get|set`, `mcp status`, `mcp serve` and `logs persist`. `clop help <command>` describes each one. Output is coloured in a terminal, `--json` prints the results as JSON, and the exit code is 0 unless every file failed (1) or the arguments were wrong (64).

- When Clop is running, `clop` sends its work to the app, so settings changes apply at once and `--gui` shows a card for a plain image optimisation. When Clop is not running, the command does the same work in its own process and reads and writes the settings file directly. Its defaults (for a settings file it creates) watch the Desktop Explorer uses, OneDrive's when Known Folder Move is on, as the app's do. Either way, files go where the placement settings say (in place, with the original kept in the working directory's backups, unless you choose otherwise), and `--output`, `--behaviour` and the template options override that for one run. URLs on the command line are downloaded and optimised into the working directory. A URL an agent passes is downloaded only from the internet: a host that resolves to this PC or the local network (loopback, private, link-local and similar addresses), also after a redirect, is refused. `clop settings set` takes a list setting as comma-separated values, or as a JSON array (`["C:\\Photos, 2026"]`) when an item has a comma, which is also how `settings get` prints such a list.
- `clop.exe` is a small launcher. It runs the installed app's own Electron as Node with `resources\cli.mjs`, so the command uses the app's image library and bundled tools, and it finds the app beside itself or through `HKCU\Software\Clop for Windows\InstallDir`. The build compiles it from `native/clop.cs` with the .NET Framework compiler that ships with Windows.
- The app and the command talk over a named pipe with a random name, which the app writes to `ipc.json` in its data folder together with a random key. Messages are lines of JSON in the shape of the macOS request and response types. Only your user account and administrators can send requests on the pipe, including when signed in to this PC over the network. Pipe names are visible to every user, so before sending anything the command asks the app to prove it knows the key; a pipe that cannot (someone else's, under the name of an app that has quit) gets nothing, and the command does the work itself. When the pipe is held but the app does not answer (busy or hung), the command still does optimisations itself but does not change settings, since the app would write over them.
- Agents (`CLOP_ORIGIN=mcp`) need `mcpEnabled` for anything that changes files or settings. They can change the settings the macOS app lets agents change, plus the pipeline library through `clop pipeline`. A pipeline with a `runScript` or `runShortcut` step, an `openWith`, `shelveWith` or `uploadWith` step that names a program by a path (any app value with a folder separator or drive), and the `Edit with` programs, need `mcpAllowScriptSteps` as well; an app named by its name does not, unless that app is an interpreter or launcher (`cmd`, `powershell`, `python`, `explorer` and the like), which agents without the permission cannot open files with. The same rule runs when Clop opens the app: headless, `clop` opens a file only with an app that has an App Paths entry, or with a program by full path for the user's own pipelines, never from a watched folder, and a packaged app needs the running Clop. Agents cannot change `mcpEnabled` or `mcpAllowScriptSteps` themselves, nor hide the tray icon (`showMenubarIcon`) or start Clop at login (`launchAtLogin`).
- Agents only name files and folders on this PC's own drives: a path that starts with a drive letter (`C:\…`, also in its long form `\\?\C:\…`). A network share (`\\server\share\…`, `\\?\UNC\…`, `file://server/…`) or a device path (`\\.\…`, any other `\\?\…`) is refused before anything looks it up, wherever an agent puts it: the files and folders of a command or tool, `--output`, the name templates, the working folder the command runs in, the `workdir` setting and the other name-template settings, watched folders and the folders pipelines are attached to, and every path in a pipeline step it runs or saves (`copy`, `move`, `rename`, `watermark`, `delete`, `runScript`, a `location`, an app). The text is checked as written and again with every `%e` and `$1`… empty (an extensionless file or a capture that matches nothing would otherwise turn `%e//host/share` into a share), and once more where the path is used, after its tokens and captures are expanded and the path is normalised: on Windows it must then be a drive-letter path, or the step, placement or command fails before anything is looked up. Looking up such a path makes Windows contact that server and offer it your credentials, and writing there sends the file off the PC. Paths the user set up stay as they are, and `clop` run by a person still takes network paths where it did before. Known limit: a drive letter mapped to a network share (`Z:\…`) looks like a local drive and cannot be told apart cheaply, so agents can still use one. Agents also cannot write to, or use files in, the folders where Clop keeps the means to put originals back (`backups`, `batch-backups` and `placing` in the working folder; a short name or link into them counts), because the next start copies back whatever a note in `placing` names, so only the notes Clop's own placements write may be there.
- Differences from macOS: `--review` reports that the batch window is not available yet. `logs persist on` turns on a log file, `logs\clop.log` in the app data folder, instead of the macOS system log. Without the app, `--async` continues in a background process, and `--copy` puts the results on the clipboard through PowerShell. `crop` with several inputs needs a folder to put the results in, as `optimise` does (macOS has no such check). `crop-pdf` and `uncrop-pdf` name each file that is missing and exit 1 when no file was edited (macOS skips missing files without a word). A forced `--portrait` or `--landscape` on `crop-pdf` follows the page as it is displayed, so a rotated page gets the orientation it shows. A card made by a command keeps the file's format whatever `defaultImageFormat` says, as the headless run and macOS do.

`clop://` links open the app: `clop://settings/<tab>[/<setting>]` or `?highlight=<setting>` opens Settings on that tab and row (see [Settings](#settings)), and `clop://mcp/start` asks before letting agents in, while `clop://mcp/stop` turns them off.

## MCP

`clop mcp serve` is the MCP server: it speaks MCP over stdin and stdout with the same 23 tools, descriptions and input schemas as macOS (`clop_optimise`, `clop_convert`, `clop_pipeline_run`, `clop_settings_set` and so on; a test compares the table with `ClopCLI/MCPToolTable.swift`). A tool goes to the running app when there is one and does the same work in its own process when there is not. Agents are held to the rules of the command line above: everything that changes files or settings needs `mcpEnabled`, and a pipeline with a script step needs `mcpAllowScriptSteps` too. `clop_start_server` asks the user in the app before allowing changes. An agent's `--output`, and the destination of a `copy`, `move` or `rename` step it runs, may not name a file that already exists; when a person's does, the file it replaces goes to the work folder's backups first.

The MCP tab of the settings window has the two switches and the agents the server can be added to: the same ones as macOS (Claude Code, Claude Desktop, Codex, Copilot CLI, pi, OpenCode and Gemini CLI first, then Antigravity, Amp, Factory Droid, Goose, Crush, Qwen Code, Kimi Code, Devin, Cursor, VS Code, VS Code Insiders and Zed). It finds the ones installed on the PC and adds or removes a `clop` entry that runs the installed `clop.exe mcp serve` by its full path. Only that entry changes: the rest of the file, comments and line endings included, stays as it was, and a copy of the file goes to `mcp-backups` in the app data folder first. A file that is not in a shape Clop can edit safely is shown, not rewritten. At launch, an entry whose command is the full path of a `clop.exe` that is gone is pointed at the current one; `clop` on PATH and `cmd /c` wrappers are left alone. A file that is not valid JSON, lists `mcpServers` (or `clop`) twice or is not UTF-8 text is shown, not rewritten. Clop also writes a server card (`mcp.json` in the app data folder and `.well-known\mcp\clop.json` in your user folder) with the start and stop commands `cmd /c start clop://mcp/start` and `cmd /c start clop://mcp/stop`.

Development builds have no `clop.exe`, so the pane shows the agents but cannot install into them.

## Batch optimiser

Dropping a folder, or more files than `batchModeFileCountThreshold` (30 by default), opens the batch window instead of one card per file, as `useBatchModeForFolders` (on by default) asks. Folders are walked with every subfolder. Images, videos, PDFs and audio files are added by their extension; hidden files, links, Clop's own files and whatever the `.clopignore-<type>` file at the top of a dropped folder names are left out. Drop more files on the window to add them, select rows and press Delete to remove them. A batch takes up to 10,000 files, and a scan stops after 200,000 folders and files of any kind; the window says so. Files that are only in the cloud (OneDrive placeholders) are skipped, since reading them would download them. Drops that overlap are added in turn, and a batch cannot start while files are still being read.

Parameter cards follow `BatchPrepView.swift` and start from the current settings:

- Images: compression (with a Lossless toggle, a Windows-only tier that changes no pixels; see [Image and clipboard details](#image-and-clipboard-details)), convert to JPEG, PNG, WebP or JPEG XL, downscale, maximum long edge, crop, adaptive PNG/JPEG and keep-if-larger for conversions.
- Video: compression, convert to MP4, HEVC, AV1 or WebM, downscale, maximum long edge, crop, encoder (adaptive, faster, smaller file, visually lossless), frame rate cap, remove audio and keep-if-larger.
- PDF: resolution (adaptive, 300 to 48 DPI, or one stop below each file's own) and an aspect-ratio crop of every page.
- Audio: compression, format (AAC, MP3, Opus, WAV, FLAC), bitrate, loudness target, cover art and its size, converting lossless inputs, and keep-if-larger.

The crop picker is the batch crop of `BatchCropButton.swift`: the saved crop sizes, the fifteen aspect ratios and a custom size, each turned portrait, landscape or adaptive (each file keeps its own orientation). An exact size is cut from every file; a width or height of 0 scales to the other side.

Results go in place, next to the original under a name template, or into a folder picked with the folder button under a name template, with the same tokens as the placement templates. A next-to-the-original template names a file; a folder template may add subfolders but cannot hold `..`, a drive or root, `:`, `%P` or `%F`, and the window cannot name a folder the chooser did not return. Two files that would land on one name are numbered (`photo-2.jpg`). Files of each type run at the width of that type's tool queue, so images, videos, PDFs and audio progress side by side; each row shows its progress, size, saving and resolution, DPI or bitrate. Cancel stops running files and skips queued ones.

Before Clop writes over a file or removes one (an in-place conversion such as PNG to JPEG), it moves it into `batch-backups\batch-<id>` with a `manifest.json` that lists every original and result. macOS clones every file up front at no cost on APFS; NTFS has no clones, so here only the files a result replaces are moved, when it replaces them. A file that changed while the batch ran is left alone and reported. A result that is not smaller leaves its file untouched. `Revert originals` gives back every original byte for byte and with its modification time, removes results saved beside them, and returns any file of yours a result took the place of. Like reverting watched folders, it leaves alone a result that was moved, deleted or edited since, and a new file where a converted original was, and reports each. The backups stay until `Delete backups`, so reverting can be repeated (a revert that stopped half way finishes on the next try; asking twice at once reverts once; after the backups are deleted it says so). Dropping files onto a finished batch that can still be reverted asks first; the older batch stays on disk, and `Open previous batch` reads its `manifest.json` back so it can still be reverted, even after a restart. The failures sheet lists each failed file with its reason and the tool's output, and copies them as text.

## Explorer integration

Right-clicking a supported file in Explorer offers `Optimise with Clop`, `Optimise (aggressive)`, `Downscale`, `Strip EXIF` and `Run pipeline...`, and `Send to` has `Optimise with Clop`. On Windows 11 they are under `Show more options` (Shift+F10 opens that menu directly): a top-level entry needs a signed sparse MSIX package, which waits for the code-signing decision. Each verb only shows for the file types it handles (`Strip EXIF` for images and videos, `Downscale` for images, videos and audio).

- The verbs are keys of the current user, `HKCU\Software\Classes\*\shell\Clop.*`. Each one starts the app itself, `"<Clop for Windows.exe>" --clop-<verb> -- "%1"` (`--clop-optimise`, `--clop-optimise-aggressive`, `--clop-downscale`, `--clop-strip-exif`, `--clop-run-pipeline`), with the file after `--` so a file named like an option stays a file; nothing goes through a shell. `Send to` runs `--clop-optimise --` with the selected files.
- Explorer starts one launch per selected file for a classic verb. Every launch reaches the one running Clop (Electron's single-instance lock passes it the arguments), which gathers the launches of a verb that arrive within a moment into one selection and handles it as a whole: the files are optimised where the optimised-file settings put them (over the file by default, as macOS Services do) with the app's own queues and cards (the same work as `clop optimise --gui`; `Downscale` takes half the size; the card places its result itself, so its Restore and `Revert last optimisations` put the original back over the file, as for a watched file), `Strip EXIF` runs on each file in turn (its original copied into the work folder's backups first and the stripped file marked as optimised, so a watched folder leaves it alone; `clop strip-exif` and an agent's `clop_strip_exif` do the same) and a notice says what was done, and `Run pipeline...` opens a menu at the pointer listing the saved pipelines that work on the files, then runs the one you pick on the files of its type, on the files themselves and not on copies (a pipeline that optimises first has its result placed by the optimised-file settings, as for a watched folder). If Clop is not running, the first launch becomes the app and the rest join it. No worker process is started. Images, PDFs and the other files that get a card share the app's queues; a downscale of video or audio runs outside them, up to four files at a time as `clop downscale --gui` does, and says "Downscaled N files" when it is done, since no card shows for it. `Send to` on a folder works on the media files directly in it. Explorer hides classic verbs above 15 selected files; `Send to` has no such limit. The portable app is a self-extracting exe, so each launch unpacks the app before it finds the one that is already running: a large selection is slower there, so the app waits longer (a second and a half, three seconds for `Run pipeline...`) for the rest of a selection before it acts.
- The installer writes the keys and the shortcut, and removes them when you uninstall (not on an update, which rewrites them). The portable app has `Add Explorer menu entries` and `Remove Explorer menu entries` in its tray menu. They run `reg.exe` from `System32` with an argument list and point at the exe you started, so keep the portable app where it is; it rewrites its entries on start only when they point at a portable build of Clop for Windows somewhere else (it was moved, or a newer build replaced the older one), and never takes over entries written by the installed app. The tray shows `Add` again if the entries point elsewhere. An install for all users still writes the entries of the account that installed it, because they live in `HKCU`, as the PATH entry does.
- `core/shell-verbs.ts` builds the whole list of keys and values (`verbPlan`) as data; removal deletes exactly the keys it names. The installer script (`native/installer.nsh`) has the same list as NSIS statements between marked lines, and a test fails when the two differ.

## Updates

The installed app checks GitHub Releases of this fork for a newer version shortly after it starts and once a day, through `electron-updater`. A new version opens a dialog: `Download and install` downloads the NSIS installer and restarts into it, `See what changed` opens the releases page. Nothing is downloaded without that choice. The portable app cannot update itself (the update feed describes the installer), so its dialog sends you to the releases page. Releases are tagged `v<version>`, and a release marked as a prerelease is a beta.

- Settings, Updates has `Check for updates` (on by default) and `Include beta versions` (off by default); both are Windows-only keys, `checkForUpdates` and `betaUpdates`. The update check is the only time Clop contacts the internet by itself. With checking off, nothing is requested, and the tray's `Check for updates…` and the `Check for updates now` button say so instead.
- Development builds and unpackaged runs never check, whatever the setting says, and neither does any system but Windows. The updater is not even loaded then (`electron/updates.ts`, `updateGate`).
- The installer is not signed. Updating from inside the app does not raise SmartScreen (the app downloads the file itself); downloading from a browser does, see [Install](#install). Update integrity rests on the SHA-512 in `latest.yml` served over HTTPS from GitHub, since electron-updater only checks an installer's signature when a publisher name is configured. The packaged app is built with Electron's fuses set so that `NODE_OPTIONS` and the `--inspect` switches have no effect and the app's `app.asar` is checked against the hash built into the exe; RunAsNode stays on, because `clop.exe` runs the command line inside the app's Electron as Node.
- `package.json` has an electron-builder `publish` section for GitHub (this repository), which makes the build write `latest.yml` and the app's update configuration. CI still only uploads the build as an artifact and runs electron-builder with `--publish never`; publishing a release is done by hand.

## Debug dump and hang detection

`About` in the tray menu and in Settings has `Save debug dump`. It writes `clop-debug-dump-<time>.zip` to `debug` in the app's data folder (the newest five are kept) and shows it in Explorer. The zip holds `app-info.txt` (versions, system, folders), `settings.json` (any setting named like a licence, email, token, secret, password or key is replaced by `<redacted>`; Clop has none today), `binaries.txt` (each bundled tool and where it was found), `recent-console.log` (the latest console lines, kept in memory even when the file log is off) and the log files of `logs` (turn on `Keep a debug log` first to get history; each file is cut to its last 5 MB). The zip also contains your settings as saved (folder paths, so your Windows user name, and saved pipelines) and the log lines, which name files. Besides keys named like secrets, only script steps are blanked: the code and path of each `runScript` step, and the whole text of a pipeline that has one. It is only a file on your PC; attach it to a bug report yourself. Nothing is sent anywhere, and there is no crash reporting.

A watchdog logs a window that stops responding (Electron's `unresponsive` and `responsive` events), a renderer that stopped, and a main process blocked for more than two seconds. For a window that stops responding it offers `Reload window` or `Keep waiting`; after `Keep waiting` that window is not asked again for a minute, and the question closes by itself if the window recovers.

## Shortcuts

The global shortcuts hold the `keyComboModifiers` (default `Ctrl+Shift`, as on macOS and in earlier Windows versions) with each key of `enabledKeys` and `quickResizeKeys`, all stored as Electron accelerators (`Control+Shift+C`). They are registered with Electron's `globalShortcut` and re-registered, only the ones that changed, when you change the keys in Settings or with `clop settings set`. They act on the newest result in view, or on the clipboard when there is none. If the newest result is still being optimised, the shortcut says to wait for it; it never acts on an older card instead.

A global shortcut is taken from every app, and Ctrl+Shift+letter is where Windows apps keep their own (Ctrl+Shift+Z redo, Ctrl+Shift+P command palettes, Ctrl+Shift+Escape Task Manager). So Windows does not copy macOS's twelve default keys: only the five that rarely collide are on (`-`, `=`, `Space`, `C`, `A`), and the others are a switch away in Settings, Keyboard Shortcuts. The rules that keep the shortcuts from breaking Windows:

- Trigger keys need two modifiers, not Ctrl+Alt on its own. Ctrl alone would take Copy, Paste and Undo from every app, Alt alone Alt+Space, Shift alone every capital letter, the Windows key alone the shell's own Win+C, Win+A and Win+Space, and Ctrl+Alt is AltGr on many keyboard layouts (it types characters such as ą and ć). Add Shift or Win to Ctrl+Alt.
- Combinations Windows keeps (`Ctrl+Shift+Escape`, `Ctrl+Alt+Delete`, `Ctrl+Esc`, `Win+L`, `Alt+Tab`, `Alt+F4`, `Win+Shift+S`) are refused wherever they could be set (the Settings window, `clop settings set`, an agent) and are never registered.
- If another program already has a combination, Clop tells you once, naming every one it could not take, and does not repeat it at later launches; the tray menu still does the action. It tries again when you change the keys and when Clop starts; it does not notice a combination another program lets go of in between.

| Key (with the trigger keys) | Action | On by default | macOS |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `C` | Optimise current clipboard | yes | C |
| `A` | Optimise the newest result, or the clipboard, more aggressively | yes | A |
| `-` | Downscale the newest result one step: a quarter off above 50%, a tenth below, on the largest step of the card menu at or below that (1, 0.7, 0.4, 0.3, 0.2, 0.1). A PDF's resolution steps down instead (from the `pdfDPI` setting when it is not adaptive), and an audio file's bitrate (from the file's own, to the next one its format allows). With no result it downscales the clipboard's result | yes | - |
| `1` to `9` | Downscale to 10% through 90% (`quickResizeKeys`) | none | 1 to 9 |
| `=` | Bring back the last result that hid or was dismissed | yes | = |
| `Space` | Open the preview window; with no result in view, the last clipboard result, or the clipboard's | yes | Space (Quick Look) |
| `X` | Speed a video or audio file up one step; with no result, the clipboard's | no | X |
| `Backspace` | Stop and dismiss the newest result | no | Delete |
| `Escape` | Stop and clear the results in view (work on watched folders that nothing shows carries on) | no | Escape |
| `K` | Open the crop window | no | K |
| `Z` | Restore the original | no | Z |
| `P` | Pause: first for the next clipboard event only, then stopped, then running again (as macOS) | no | P |

Step state follows the card too: restoring a result, or choosing a downscale from its menu, moves where the next shortcut step starts. `Ctrl+Shift+Escape` is Task Manager, so Escape cannot be switched on with the default trigger keys; change them first. `R` (rename) has no Windows action, since cards have no inline file name field; the key is kept in the setting (an imported macOS list keeps it) but the Settings window does not list it. Nothing in Clop opens a preview for `-` or `1` to `9`; only the tray's `Downscale and preview the clipboard` does.

Without the trigger keys, a card answers a few single keys while it has keyboard focus and no text field is active: `1` through `9` resize an image to 10% through 90%, `-` steps it down, `C` copies, `R` restores, and `Delete`, `Space`, `D`, `K`, `S`, `A` dismiss, preview, compare, crop, save as and optimise aggressively (see [Result cards](#result-cards-right-click-menus-and-the-compact-list)). `Escape` hides corner cards.

## Development and verification

The Windows app uses Electron, TypeScript, React and Sharp, with a small STA helper using built-in Windows PowerShell 5.1 and .NET Framework. It captures mouse-press coordinates, hit-tests UI Automation file items in Explorer and MSAA image objects in other apps, handles drag-end events and watches clipboard sequence numbers. A background MTA thread checks accessibility objects, leaving the mouse hook's message pump responsive. Completed checks carry a gesture generation so releasing or cancelling a drag discards a late answer. The mouse hook queues coordinates without doing COM work or reading clipboard data. The renderer is sandboxed, has no Node.js access and receives a narrow preload API. External navigation is blocked.

Use Node.js 24 or newer. From `windows/`:

```sh
npm ci
node scripts/fetch-tools.mjs
npm run desktop
npm test
npm run build
npm run dist:win
```

`scripts/fetch-tools.mjs` downloads the command-line tools pinned by version and sha256 in `scripts/tools.json`, verifies each download and copies the needed files into `.tools/win32-x64/bin`. Packages already present at the pinned hash are skipped. Only an x64 set exists; on ARM64 Windows the x64 tools run under emulation, so the script and the app use the x64 set there too. The installer ships that folder as `resources/bin`. On Linux and macOS the script does nothing unless you pass `--platform win32`; development there uses `ffmpeg`, `gs`, `gifsicle`, `gifski`, `jpegoptim`, `pngquant`, `exiftool`, `heif-dec`, `heif-enc`, `cjxl` and `djxl` from `PATH`. `CLOP_TOOLS_DIR` overrides both. Tests that need a missing tool are skipped locally and fail in CI.

Settings are stored in `settings.json` in the app's data folder (`%APPDATA%\Clop for Windows`). `core/settings/schema.ts` defines every key with its type, default and description. The store uses the same key names as `Clop/Settings.swift`, but a few values use Windows encodings, such as shortcut keys, encoder names and app paths; the schema notes each one. Folder defaults are stored relative to the user profile (`~/Desktop`) and expanded when used. Keys for features that exist only on macOS stay in the schema but are marked unsupported. `node scripts/extract-mac-settings.mjs` prints the macOS key names, and a test checks the schema covers them all. Settings saved by earlier Windows versions are converted to the new keys at the next launch.

`core/data/` holds the macOS app's default crop sizes and aspect ratios and its iPhone and iPad screen sizes. Tests compare each table with the Swift source it comes from.

The packaging command runs on Windows, fetches the tools and produces NSIS and portable executables in `release/`. On Windows, `npm run build` also bundles the CLI as `dist-electron/cli.mjs` and compiles `clop.exe`. CI runs every bundled tool with only the bundle and Windows on `PATH`, checks that the bundle carries every DLL its programs import, tests image processing, compiles the Windows helper, checks native clipboard formats and launches the packaged app, including an Explorer verb launch (the app started again with `--clop-optimise -- <file>`) that must reach the running app and optimise two files, one named like an option. An external clipboard write must produce a corner result automatically, and so must an MP4, a PDF and an MP3 copied as files, whose optimised files must then be on the clipboard. An image copied by an ignored app must not, and a video and an image dropped into a watched folder in the test profile must be optimised in place with their originals in `backups`. CI checks the 196 × 166 card, selected format, resize, restore and clipboard loop protection, and saves an actual Windows screenshot. `scripts/cli-smoke.mjs` then runs the packaged `clop.exe` with the app closed and again with it open. The smoke runs turn update checks off. Real mouse gestures in a separate native app and Explorer verify image drags, release/Escape and suppression of text selection, text drags, unsupported images, empty space, window movement and resizing.

`npm run dev` serves a development-only corner-card preview on loopback port 5274. Its background is a neutral canvas so the transparent cards can be inspected in a browser. Paste an image to inspect a card. There are no browse or sample controls. A browser cannot demonstrate system-wide clipboard watching or native Windows drag detection; those are exercised by the packaged-app test. Preview data stays under `.preview-data/` and can be removed after shutdown. The same server shows the settings window at `/?preferences=1` (add `&tab=<tab>`, `&highlight=<row>` or `&search=<words>`), with the settings kept in memory; pickers answer that they need the desktop app.

The desktop app does not upload files or send analytics. The installed and portable apps ask GitHub Releases for a new version unless `checkForUpdates` is off, and development runs never do. A link is only downloaded when it is explicitly dropped onto the target, copied and optimised with `Ctrl+Shift+C`, or passed to `clop`. Managed machines may block the Windows helper; Clop reports the failure, and save and drag operations remain available. Without the helper, automatic clipboard optimisation and watched folders do nothing until Clop restarts, because neither can tell a cloud-only OneDrive file or a private copy from an ordinary one; watched folders say so once.

In the installed app, `resources\bin\THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.txt` lists every bundled package with its version, licence, homepage, exact download address and the address of its corresponding source code. Their licence texts and notices are in `resources\bin\licenses\<package>`. `fetch-tools.mjs` produces both from `scripts/tools.json`. Ghostscript and gifski are AGPLv3; they run as separate programs, and section 13 of GPLv3 permits combining GPLv3 and AGPLv3 work.

### Refreshing pinned tools

To update a tool, change its entry in `scripts/tools.json`: the version, the URL, the sha256 of the download, the archive paths in `files` and `licenses`, and the `sources` addresses for that exact version. Where an archive has no licence text, `licenseTexts` pins one by URL and sha256. Then run `node scripts/fetch-tools.mjs --platform win32`, which refuses a download whose hash differs. repo.msys2.org removes old package versions, so the MSYS2 entries eventually stop downloading. Regenerate them with `npx tsx scripts/resolve-msys2.ts libheif:heif-dec,heif-enc pngquant:pngquant`. It reads the current MSYS2 package database, follows the tools' DLL imports to every package they load and prints their entries with sha256, licence files and MSYS2 source package addresses; replace the `msys2-*` entries with its output. BtbN keeps its month-end FFmpeg builds for about two years; pick a newer month-end `autobuild-*` release when the pinned one disappears. CI checks that each tool reports its pinned version, that the bundle carries every DLL its programs import, and that every package has licence texts and a notice with its source address.
